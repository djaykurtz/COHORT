A TEAM OF PERSPECTIVES / EVERY CONTROL HAS A REASON

Keep the reason. Keep the responsibility.

The architect asks what should own a fact. The PM asks how work is broken down and adopted. Builders make the change; independent reviewers ask which revision was checked; an analyst asks whether the operator can see what happened. Authored roles and personas make those perspectives explicit.

The controls prevent concrete failures: two nodes taking the same work, an approval applied to the wrong artifact, rationale disappearing after a handoff, or an unknown result reported as done. A narrow defect may be a SWAT; work under an accepted design may be a task; a new cross-system contract can require an RFC. Not every task needs a new design process.

Saved research and decision history turns into team conventions that get reused. Authority, ownership and review still come from the records that own them, so a bigger memory never quietly grants permission.

01 / RETRIEVAL BEFORE REUSE

Find the evidence. Inspect its owner.

Spyglass makes research and knowledge searchable through a derived index. Cairn preserves the source records, revisions and rationale. A retrieved snippet is a starting point; changing current guidance still goes through its owner.

02 / THE TEAM WEIGHS IN, THEN THE DESIGN IS SET

Every specialty shapes the design.

An RFC is a design proposal the whole team weighs in on. Each node responds from its role and the projects it usually leads, and can react to what the others said. Concerns stay on the record and carry into the next round, the way a real design review works.

The PM sums up each round, and that summary carries the most weight. The operator has the final say, approving the design as ready to build once the comments and considerations cover enough ground. There's no head count, but strong pushback carries real weight. Building and reviewing the code come afterwards as separate steps.

03 / AUTHORITY BEFORE ACTIVITY

Every claim has a single winner.

Agents can announce the same work at the same time. COHORT's documented contract puts a single-winner compare-and-swap in the authoritative store. Losing a race must be free: no ownership, no work-in-progress charge, and an explicit loss result.
